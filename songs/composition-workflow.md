### Composition Workflows — Theory Definitions & Comprehensive Frameworks
Oct 19, 2026

#### Scope
A complete, source-grounded compositional methodology comprising six workflows and six entry-point variants. This document provides a pure music theory and technical songwriting framework governing every part of composition—from initial intent to final structural revision.

The workflows are not fixed stages. Concept and Form always open, Arrangement always closes, and the three composing workflows (Melody, Harmony, Groove) run in whatever order the chosen entry-point variant sets. The default sequence is optimized for vocal songs. Instrumental, electronic, or groove-driven compositions use the appropriate entry-point variant, treating lyric-specific and prosodic items as optional while retaining mandatory structural and harmonic requirements.

---

#### Core Compositional Principles

1. **Intent Before Technique**: Every musical, harmonic, and rhythmic choice must serve a pre-defined central idea and emotional target. Technical devices are tools to execute emotional intent, never ends in themselves.
2. **Macro-Structure Decided Early**: Song form, section contrast, tonal strategies, and cadential punctuation are established during framing, preventing structural collapse during arrangement.
3. **Cadences Function as Structural Punctuation**: Open cadences (ending on V, IV, or vi) create forward momentum and suspense at the end of verses and prechoruses; closed cadences (V7 → I) deliver resolution and stability at choruses and refrains.
4. **Melodic Independence & Outline Focus**: Melodies possess independent architectural integrity defined by a melodic outline and step progression, shaping or co-existing with harmony rather than merely playing chord tones.
5. **Rhythm Precedes Pitch**: A melodic motif is conceived as a rhythmic pattern before pitches are assigned, ensuring structural drive and syncopated vitality.
6. **Arrangement Regulates Energy, Not Structure**: The Arrangement workflow manages density, layering, timbre, and tessitura; it does not fix structural or harmonic defects.
7. **Rewriting is Systematic Writing**: Composition culminates in objective revision passes against explicit music theory benchmarks until all criteria pass.

---

#### How the Workflows Connect

Each workflow has goals, execution steps, outputs, and an exit condition. A workflow is complete when its exit condition is met; the entry-point variant decides which workflow runs next.

```
                 +-----------+        +-----------+
   (always) ---> |  Concept  | -----> |   Form    |
                 +-----------+        +-----------+
                                            |
              +-----------------------------+-----------------------------+
              |                             |                             |
              v                             v                             v
        +-----------+                 +-----------+                 +-----------+
        |  Melody   | <-------------> |  Harmony  | <-------------> |  Groove   |
        +-----------+                 +-----------+                 +-----------+
              |      any order, set by the entry-point variant            |
              +-----------------------------+-----------------------------+
                                            |
                                            v
                                      +-------------+
                                      | Arrangement |
                                      +-------------+
```

Three ordering rules apply to every variant:

1. **Concept and Form always open.** The Lyrics First variant inserts a lyric draft between them.
2. **Melody, Harmony, and Groove run in the variant's order.** Each one is written to fit whatever material already exists. When a later workflow forces a change, recheck the exit conditions of the earlier ones.
3. **Arrangement runs only after Melody, Harmony, and Groove are complete.**

##### Workflow: Concept & Emotional Intent
* **Goal**: Establish the central thematic thesis and emotional boundaries of the composition before writing notes.
* **Execution**:
  1. Define the central idea in a single declarative sentence. For vocal songs, fix the song title or refrain hook.
  2. Map the emotional target on three psychological axes:
     * *Evaluation*: Positive vs. Negative.
     * *Potency*: Strong/Assertive vs. Gentle/Vulnerable.
     * *Activity*: Driving/High-Energy vs. Calm/Static.
  3. Determine lyrical narrative perspective (e.g., Direct Address, Storyteller, Internal Monologue).
* **Output**: A one-line concept statement, fixed title/hook line, and a 3-axis emotional profile.
* **Exit Condition**: Every subsequent melodic, harmonic, and structural choice can be directly evaluated against the emotional profile.

##### Workflow: Pre-Selections, Macro-Form & Tonal Strategy
* **Goal**: Construct the structural scaffold and plan sectional contrast before composing phrases.
* **Execution**:
  1. Select meter (e.g., 4/4, 3/4, 6/8), tempo (BPM), primary scale/mode (e.g., Major/Ionian, Natural Minor/Aeolian, Mixolydian, Dorian, Major/Minor Pentatonic).
  2. Choose the macro-song form (e.g., AABA, ABAB, Verse/Chorus ABABCB, Verse/Prechorus/Chorus ABABCBB, Verse/Refrain).
  3. Select the primary **Tonal Strategy**:
     * *Strategy A (Functional Cadential Payoff)*: Establish clear tonal center in verse, create harmonic tension/instability in prechorus (avoiding tonic I), and deliver a strong closed cadence arrival on I in chorus.
     * *Strategy B (Modal/Unstable Start to Tonic Payoff)*: Begin in an unstable or relative modal area (e.g., Aeolian or Dorian), introduce harmonic hints, and resolve to the true relative major tonic at the chorus.
     * *Strategy C (Static Loop / Tonal Ambiguity)*: Use a continuous 4-chord loop (e.g., vi–IV–I–V or ii–IV–I–V) across all sections, achieving contrast entirely through melodic tessitura, rhythmic density, phrase length shifts, and modal shifts.
  4. Build a **Sectional Contrast Map** specifying targets for each section:
     * *Tessitura/Register*: Verses sit lower; Choruses sit at peak vocal/instrumental register.
     * *Rhythmic Density*: Verses conversational/busy; Choruses declarative with longer held tones or syncopated hooks.
     * *Harmonic Stability & Rhythm*: Verses less stable (faster/slower chord changes, open cadences); Choruses stable (clear cadences, held tonal anchors).
* **Output**: A comprehensive Form Map detailing section lengths (bars), energy levels, tonal strategies, and contrast parameters.
* **Exit Condition**: Every section has a defined bar length, energy target, and explicit contrast parameters with adjacent sections.

##### Workflow: Melodic Motives, Prosody & Melodic Outline
* **Goal**: Compose memorable section melodies using rhythm-first motif development, strong prosody, and structured step progressions.
* **Inputs**: Concept and Form Map; any existing harmony or groove, which the melody must fit without losing its independence.
* **Execution**:
  1. **Rhythmic Motif Development**: Draft rhythmic patterns (e.g., $a, a, a, x$ or $a, a, b, a$) before pitch assignment. Utilize front-heavy phrases for momentum or back-heavy phrases for relaxed, conversational delivery.
  2. **Pitch Assignment & Phrase Architecture**:
     * Group motives into 8-bar *Periods* (4-bar Antecedent question ending open, 4-bar Consequent answer ending closed) or 8-bar *Sentences* (Statement, Restatement, Continuation, Cadential Close).
     * Keep vocal phrases to 5–10 notes (1.5–4 seconds) separated by functional rests for breathing and clarity.
  3. **Melodic Outline & Step Progression**:
     * Establish a *melodic outline* tracking structural notes on strong beats (beats 1 and 3 in 4/4), held notes, and phrase extremes.
     * Embed a continuous stepwise *melodic step progression* (e.g., $\hat{1}\to\hat{2}\to\hat{3}$ or $\hat{8}\to\hat{7}\to\hat{6}$) beneath surface leaps to give long-range directional motion across 8 to 16 bars.
  4. **Interval Dynamics & Leap Recovery**: Recover large disjunct leaps (6ths, octaves) immediately by stepwise motion in the opposite direction.
  5. **Climax Placement**: Place the absolute highest pitch (climax note) in the chorus, ideally at the 66%–75% structural golden ratio point.
  6. **Prosody Alignment**: Place natural speech-stressed syllables on metrically strong downbeats. Align lyrical rhyme schemes with musical phrase structures, using perfect or slant rhymes as counterpoint to balance asymmetric phrases.
* **Output**: Monophonic lead sheet / melody lines for all sections with documented melodic step progressions.
* **Exit Condition**: The lead melody is emotionally expressive and structurally complete when sung or played entirely alone without chordal accompaniment.

##### Workflow: Harmonization, Cadences, Voice Leading & Dissonance
* **Goal**: Build functional or modal harmony—around the melody if it exists, or as the harmonic bed a later melody will be written over—execute cadential punctuation, apply background lines, and manage dissonance resolution.
* **Inputs**: Concept and Form Map (tonal strategy, cadence plan); any existing melody or groove.
* **Execution**:
  1. **Progression Selection**: Select chord progressions based on root motion classification:
     * *Fifth Progressions*: Strongest functional drive (e.g., $ii\to V\to I$, $IV\to V\to I$).
     * *Third Progressions*: Smooth, soft transitions (e.g., $I\to vi\to IV$, $I\to iii$).
     * *Second Progressions*: Stepwise energy and linear motion (e.g., $IV\to V$, $I\to ii$).
     * *Chromatic Progressions*: Temporary exit from the diatonic scale for ear-grabbing color.
  2. **Cadential Punctuation**:
     * Verses & Prechoruses: End on open cadences ($V$, $IV$, or $vi$) or deceptive cadences ($V\to vi$, $V\to IV$) to preserve forward momentum.
     * Choruses & Refrains: End on closed cadences ($V^7\to I$ or $iv\to I$), with the melody landing firmly on tonic ($\hat{1}$) or stable chord tones ($\hat{3}, \hat{5}$).
  3. **Voice Leading & Dissonance Management**:
     * *Prepare-Strike-Resolve (PSR)*: Prepare dissonant 7ths as consonant chord tones in the preceding chord, strike on the weak/strong beat, and resolve down by step into the target chord.
     * *Five Alternate Resolution Patterns* (when strict downward PSR is melodically unfeasible):
       1. *Diatonic Passing Introduction*: Approach the 7th diatonically from above or below as a passing tone.
       2. *Deferred Resolution*: Insert a defer tone (chordal or non-chordal) before final resolution ($Prepare \to Strike \to Defer \to Resolve$).
       3. *Transferred Resolution*: Transfer the resolving voice to another register or vocal part (e.g., Alto to Soprano).
       4. *Passive Resolution*: Hold the 7th as it becomes a consonant chord tone in the succeeding chord.
       5. *Secondary Melodic Resolution*: Resolve active scale degrees upward (e.g., $\hat{7}\to\hat{1}$ or $\hat{4}\to\hat{3}$) to drive melodic energy.
  4. **Extended Voice Leading & Background Lines**:
     * *3-7 Technique*: Build two-part background countermelodies using exclusively the 3rd and 7th scale degrees of each chord, defining the harmonic skeleton.
     * *Substitutions*: Apply tritone substitution ($bV^7$ for $V^7$), 9-for-1 root substitution, or altered dominants ($7b9, 7\#11, 7b13$) for heightened tension.
* **Output**: Harmonized lead sheets (or chord charts, if no melody exists yet) for each section with explicit chord symbols, voice-leading annotations, and background 3-7 lines.
* **Exit Condition**: All section boundaries possess correct cadential punctuation, and all chordal/non-chordal dissonances follow verified resolution paths.

##### Workflow: Rhythm Section, Bass & Linear Counterpoint
* **Goal**: Construct the rhythm section foundation, lock the kick-bass interface, and integrate linear counterpoint that respects top-line space.
* **Inputs**: Form Map energy targets; any existing melody or harmony.
* **Execution**:
  1. **Groove & Velocity Profile**: Set drum groove, subdivision feel (straight vs. swing), and velocity dynamics matching the Form Map energy targets.
  2. **Bass Line Architecture**:
     * Anchor root movement on strong downbeats.
     * Lock bass rhythmic accents directly to the kick drum.
     * Utilize inversion pass-throughs (e.g., $I\to V/3\to vi$) to create continuous stepwise bass movement.
  3. **Linear Counterpoint & Riff Interaction**:
     * Ensure the bass line and secondary instrumental riffs move in contrary or oblique motion relative to the primary vocal lead line.
     * Write instrumental riffs to target "dead spots" (vocal rests or held sustained notes) in the vocal melody, avoiding rhythmic crowding during active vocal phrases. If no melody exists yet, leave planned dead spots and recheck them once the melody is written.
* **Output**: Fully orchestrated rhythm section tracks (Drums, Bass, Guitars/Keys/Riffs).
* **Exit Condition**: The bass and rhythm section solidify the groove and harmonic foundation without masking or competing with the lead vocal melody.

---

##### Workflow: Arrangement & Dynamic Energy Curve
* **Goal**: Shape the micro- and macro-dynamics, manage instrument density over time, and build smooth sectional transitions.
* **Inputs**: Completed Melody, Harmony, and Groove workflows; Form Map energy targets.
* **Execution**:
  1. **Dynamic Energy Curve**: Map instrumentation layering across the timeline to achieve a progressive crescendo toward the final chorus.
  2. **Instrumentation Layering & Register Shifts**:
     * Introduce or subtract instruments at section boundaries (e.g., drop bass in verse 2, add high synth pad in chorus 2).
     * Shift instrument voicings into higher octaves in choruses to reinforce tessitura contrast.
  3. **Transitions & Fills**: Insert drum fills, sweep effects, or pickup notes in the final bar of sections to signal upcoming arrivals.
  4. **Intro & Outro Optimization**: Keep radio song introductions under 10 seconds (truncating or using an instrumental hook). Design outro codas with repeat-and-fade or definite cadential endings.
* **Output**: Complete multitrack arrangement map following the Form Map energy targets.
* **Exit Condition**: Energy levels throughout the full arrangement match the Form Map targets without introducing unauthorized structural changes.

#### Entry-Point Variants

Different creative sparks require distinct entry points. Every variant opens with Concept and Form and closes with Arrangement; the variants differ only in the order of Melody, Harmony, and Groove.

| Variant | Primary Style / Context | Sequence | Critical Guidelines & Traps |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Melody First** *(Default)* | Vocal Pop, Ballads, Memorable Lead Lines | **Concept → Form → Melody → Harmony → Groove → Arrangement** | Ensure chords support the melody rather than forcing the melody into cliché triads. |
| **Title / Hook First** | Commercial Pop, High-Concept Songwriting | **Concept (fix title) → Form → Melody (hook first) → Harmony → Groove → Arrangement** | The central title/hook line must land on the main downbeat of the chorus arrival. |
| **Lyrics First** | Storyteller, Folk, Hip-Hop, Poetic Songs | **Concept → Lyric Draft → Form → Melody → Harmony → Groove → Arrangement** | Vocal melody rhythm must strictly reflect natural lyric speech stress (prosody). |
| **Groove / Riff First** | Funk, Dance, Rock, Instrumental | **Concept → Form → Groove → Melody → Harmony → Arrangement** | Riffs easily crowd lead melodies; keep riff registers and rhythmic dead spots distinct from vocal lines. |
| **Harmony First** | Jazz, Electronic, Mood-Driven Songs | **Concept → Form → Harmony → Melody → Groove → Arrangement** | Melodies risk becoming static chord-tone outlines; compose melodic rhythms before assigning pitches. |
| **Bass Line First** | Funk, R&B, Contrapuntal Compositions | **Concept → Form → Groove (bass) → Harmony → Melody → Arrangement** | Bass sets both root motion and counter-rhythm; verify bass-melody voice leading and cadences. |
