# Break My Soul — Deep Crying Salsa Arrangement Outline (v2, slow grid)

Oct 22, 2026 · @leonardo soares silva

## Concept and tempo

The arrangement stays locked at 115 BPM (quarter note, 4/4), the original's tempo, so it drops straight into a DJ set. The 2-3 son clave spans two bars, which makes this a slow salsa / son-bolero feel, not uptempo salsa.

| Parameter | Value |
| --- | --- |
| Tempo | 115 BPM quarter note, fixed grid, no tempo changes |
| Clave | 2-3 son clave, implied by bass, piano and horns (not played), across two bars of 4/4 (one cycle = 4.17 s) |
| Subdivision | 8th-note grid; no 16th-note pushes |
| Feel | Salsa lenta / son-bolero; roughly 35–40% slower than typical salsa romántica (170–190 BPM in this notation) |
| Key | G# minor (horn parts written in A♭ minor); final coros in A minor |
| Length | 92 bars, about 3:12; montuno and final coros extend in 4-bar units |

The slow clave cycle is the point: it leaves room for long vocal phrases and sustained chords, which is where the crying comes from. Energy comes from density (horns, coro, montuno), not speed. There are no drums or percussion anywhere in the arrangement.

## Form and energy

Eight sections, every one starting on an odd bar, so each phrase begins on the two-side of the clave. Energy rises, collapses into a breakdown, then peaks in the new key.

| Section | Bars | Length | Energy (1–5) | Role |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Intro (half-time bolero) | 1–8 | 8 | 1 | Voice, piano, strings; no clave |
| Verse (canción) | 9–24 | 16 | 2 | Chromatic line cliché; the crying section |
| Build | 25–32 | 8 | 3 | Circle of fourths into the montuno |
| Montuno / coro | 33–48 | 16 | 4 | Title hook as coro, original pregones |
| Mambo | 49–64 | 16 | 4 | Trombone-led horn mambo |
| Breakdown | 65–72 | 8 | 2 | Piano and coro only; pivot to A minor |
| Final coros (A minor) | 73–88 | 16 | 5 | Half-step lift, full band |
| Cierre | 89–92 | 4 | 1 | Tutti hit, piano echoes the verse cliché |

The intro uses a half-time feel (57.5 BPM felt, 115 on the grid), so it sounds rubato but stays beat-matched. Live, the cierre can end on a fermata; for DJ use, let the last chord ring over the grid.

## Voicings

The bass owns every root, so piano and horns never double it in the low register. Keep piano left-hand voicings above C3.

### Line cliché (verse)

One voice moves; everything else holds G#–B–D#.

| Bar | Chord | Moving voice |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 9 | G#m9 | G# (root) |
| 10 | G#m(maj7) | F𝄪 (major 7th) |
| 11 | G#m7 | F# (minor 7th) |
| 12 | G#m6 | E# (major 6th) |
| 13 | Emaj7 | E (bass arrives on the new root) |

The hook's F# rubs against F𝄪 in bar 10 as a major 7th above the bass if the cliché is in the bass. Keep it in an inner voice for verse 1; let it rub in verse 2.

### Tritone substitution (A7(#11) → G#m)

Every voice moves by step: A → G# in the bass, E → D#, G → G#, C# → B. D#7 and A7 share the same guide tones (F𝄪/G and C#), which is why the swap works.

### D#7alt, rootless piano voicing

- **Left hand:** F𝄪 (3rd) and C# (♭7), a tritone shell.
- **Right hand, alt colour:** B major triad (B–D#–F#) = ♭13, root, #9.
- **Right hand, ♭9 colour:** A major triad (A–C#–E) = #11, ♭7, ♭9.

### One upper structure for both dominants (horns)

The B major triad sits on top of D#7 as ♭13–root–#9 and on top of A7 as 9–#11–13. In build bars 30–31, trumpets hold B–D#–F# and trombones hold G and C# while only the bass moves D# → A. The horns sound frozen and the floor drops out from under them. Into G#m, the trumpets keep B and D# and lift F# to G#.

### Intro colour

Over G#m(add9), use the quartal stack D#–G#–C#–F#–A# (G#m11). It's open and dark without sounding like jazz comping.

### Piano montuno

From the build onward, play the montuno with both hands in octaves and chord tones between the thumbs. No drop-2 shells under it; the bass and the octaves already fill the space.

## Rhythm section

No drums or percussion: the bass, piano and horns keep time on their own. The 2-3 son clave is still the grid, spread over two bars, but nobody plays it. Odd bars are the two-side, even bars the three-side.

```
Beat:       1 & 2 & 3 & 4 & | 1 & 2 & 3 & 4 & |
2-3 clave:  . . X . X . . . | X . . X . . X . |   (implied)
Bass:       . . . X . . X ~ | ~ . . X . . X ~ |
```

- **Bass tumbao:** the "and" of 2 and beat 4, with beat 4 tied over the barline. Beat 4 plays the next chord's root early. With no drums, this is the main pulse, so play it steady and slightly forward.
- **Piano:** anticipates chord changes on the "and" of 4, a half beat after the bass. From the build onward the montuno is the time-keeper: no rubato, and accent the three-side's "and" of 2 and beat 4 so the clave is audible.
- **Horns:** mambo stabs land on clave strokes, reinforcing the grid the percussion would normally carry.
- **Chord changes:** written on beat 1 of each bar; bass and piano anticipate them as above. No harmonic changes on beat 2.
